## Where this actually stands

Nothing is being collected. No signature, no pledge, no address, no money.
No version of this draft has been filed with the European Commission and
none has been registered, which you can check for yourself on the
[versions](/law/versions) page rather than taking it from this one.

What the project needs at this stage is not supporters. It is arguments, and
it is people willing to be named. A citizens' initiative is filed by a group
of at least seven people resident in at least seven different Member States,
and their names are published by the Commission. Seven such people do not
exist yet. Until they do, this is a draft with one editor, and the honest
description of its position is that it has not been tested by anyone who
disagrees with it in public.

## Three things that help, in order of how much

**Argue with the text.** This is worth more than agreement and there is no
close second. The draft has been attacked by adversarial review at every
gate and by exactly one human, who wrote it. Every objection on this site
was written by the project against itself, which is a method with an obvious
ceiling. If you can find the hole, the answer this file needs is the hole.
Open an [issue or pull
request](https://github.com/ownthemachine/own-the-machine/issues) and the
argument is public from the first sentence, where it can be read by everyone
the draft concerns.

**Be one of the seven.** If you are a citizen of the Union, of the age to
vote in European Parliament elections, and you would consider putting your
name to a filing, say so. You are not agreeing to anything today, for the
reason set out below.

**Connect it to an organisation.** A union body, an academic institute, a
foundation, an NGO with standing on automation, tax or capital ownership. An
introduction is worth more than an endorsement, and a hostile reading from a
serious institution is worth more than either.

## What being an organiser means, stated plainly

The rules are the Commission's, not this project's, and the authoritative
version is at [europa.eu](https://citizens-initiative.europa.eu/), not here.
In outline: a group of organisers is at least seven natural persons, each
resident in a different Member State, each old enough to vote in European
Parliament elections. The group names a representative and a substitute. The
names of the organisers are published in the Commission's public register.
Members of the European Parliament do not count towards the seven.

What it does **not** mean, at least not yet:

- **It is not a commitment.** This project has published gates with kill
  criteria. Nobody is asked to be an organiser until the registrability
  question is answered, and if the answer is bad the file is published as a
  model law and the initiative stops. Saying you would consider it costs you
  nothing and commits you to nothing.
- **It is not an endorsement of every article.** An organiser files a text;
  an organiser does not have to have written it or to like all of it. If you
  would file it only with changes, that is a more useful message than
  agreement, and it belongs in the open where the changes can be argued.
- **It is not fundraising.** There is nothing to give to. The funding
  position is on the [about](/about) page and it is that there is no
  organisation, no account and no donations.

## How to say so

Two routes, and the public one is better.

**In the open**, which is preferred because the answer is then readable by
everyone else weighing the same question: open an issue on the
[repository](https://github.com/ownthemachine/own-the-machine/issues) and
say which Member State you are in, what you would bring, and whether you
would consider being named.

**By email**, if the question is genuinely personal or you would rather not
be public before you have decided: write to
[hello@ownthemachine.eu](mailto:hello@ownthemachine.eu). It helps to say the
same three things.

Either way, the most useful message anyone can send is the one that begins
"this will not work, because".

## What happens to what you send

An issue is public, permanently, and written by you, so it contains exactly
what you chose to put in it.

An email reaches the editor and nothing else. It is not added to a list,
because there is no list. It is not forwarded, not published, and not used
to send you anything you did not ask for. If a group of organisers is ever
constituted, whoever is in it will have said so themselves, in writing, at
that point and not before. The controller and your rights are described on
the [about](/about) page.
