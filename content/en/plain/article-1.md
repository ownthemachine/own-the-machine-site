L0: Sets out what the rules do, who they cover, and how big the citizens' stake is meant to become.

This article sets out the plan of the whole Regulation, and now also its
measure of success. It creates a procedure for deciding which companies are
covered and obliges those companies to issue a citizens' capital warrant (a
right to future shares) to a new European fund, the Reserve. It states the
scale the participation is meant to reach: within a generation, the holdings
behind each citizen's entitlement should be worth in the order of six months
of the median disposable income in the EU, and the Commission must report on
progress and propose changing the percentage or the thresholds, in either
direction, if the objective is clearly out of reach or clearly exceeded.
Payments to citizens remain what follows from owning the stake, never the
purpose of the rules. It establishes the Reserve itself, gives EU citizens a
personal right to payments administered through bodies in each country, and
protects both the fund and that right. The rules apply to any company
offering goods or services in the EU market, no matter where in the world
it is based.
