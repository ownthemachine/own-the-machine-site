L0: Gives every adult EU citizen an equal automatic share of the Reserve's payouts, which cannot be sold, seized or signed away; but the law does not promise a payout every year, only at least once every three years.

If you are an EU citizen aged 18 or over, you hold this entitlement: a personal right to an equal share of every distribution the Reserve declares. It arises automatically. There is no application and no test of income, wealth, work or contribution history; you only register with a national vehicle (the body in your country that runs the accounts) so you can be paid. Every holder's share is equal. The right itself cannot be sold, pledged, seized or surrendered, but money already credited to your account is your property and can be inherited. The Reserve does not pay out every year. It declares a distribution only in a year in which the amount available would give each holder at least ten times the average cost of making one payment to them; otherwise the money is held back rather than paid out. This is deliberate, not a shortfall: sending every holder a few cents costs more to process than the payment is worth, so a payment that small destroys value instead of delivering it, and the law lets the amount build up in the Reserve rather than be spent on the cost of sending it. That means there can be years with no payment to you at all. The backstop is strict: whatever the amount, the Reserve must still pay out at least once in every three calendar years in which it has anything to distribute. In a year with no payment, the Reserve must publish what each holder would have received and the value held in the Reserve per holder, so you can see what is being kept for you.
