L0: Defines the key terms: which companies count, what the warrant is, which events turn it into shares, and two new terms that stop a company dodging it by paying itself instead of selling itself.

This article fixes the meaning of the words the rest of the rules rely on. A 'covered undertaking' is a designated company, counted together with its whole corporate group. 'Automated cognitive services' are services performed largely by software doing work that would otherwise need human thinking. The 'citizens' capital warrant' is the instrument giving the Reserve the right to buy new shares in a designated company. A 'liquidity event' is a stock-market listing, a change of control or sales adding up to at least 20 % of the company's shares within 12 months. Two further terms were added because a company that never sells itself can still pay itself: 'shareholder extraction' is the value a company pays out to its owners and to people connected with them, whether as dividends, buybacks, capital returned, loan repayments to insiders or fees, interest and royalties paid to insiders above what an outside party would charge. 'Automated assets' are the software, models, computing infrastructure, data and intellectual property that its automated services depend on: the things a company would have to hand over to keep the underlying business out of the Reserve's reach while it goes on paying itself. Article 5 uses both terms to decide when the warrant turns into shares even without a sale, and who owes it if those assets are moved elsewhere. The article itself imposes no obligations.
