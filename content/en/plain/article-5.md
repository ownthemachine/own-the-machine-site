L0: Requires each covered company to give the Reserve the right to buy 3 % of its shares cheaply, triggered by a sale or listing, by heavy payouts to its owners, or by seven years passing, whichever comes first.

Within three months of designation, a covered company must issue the citizens' capital warrant to the Reserve. The warrant turns into shares on whichever of three kinds of event happens first. The first is a liquidity event: a stock-market listing, a change of control, or sales adding up to at least 20 % of the company's shares within 12 months. The second is heavy extraction: if, over any three consecutive financial years, the value the company pays out to its owners and to people connected with them (dividends, buybacks, capital returned, loans repaid to insiders, or fees, interest and royalties paid to insiders above what an outside party would charge) exceeds 25 % of its turnover from the covered automated services, the warrant turns into shares even though nothing has been sold. The third is a seven-year long-stop: if none of the above has happened within seven years of the warrant being issued, it turns into shares anyway. Whichever trigger fires, the Reserve may then subscribe for newly issued shares equal to 3 % of the company's fully diluted capital, paying only their nominal value (a token price).

The shares the Reserve receives cannot be shares of a lesser sort. If the company later creates other classes of shares, the Reserve's shares must rank at least as well as the best of those new classes for dividends, other payouts and what happens on a sale or winding up, though this never jumps the Reserve ahead of the company's creditors. Any arrangement designed to water down or defeat this right, such as issuing shares that rank ahead of the Reserve's, does not work against the Reserve, even though it stays valid between the people who made it. Neither the warrant nor the shares it turns into carry votes or any say in management for as long as the Reserve holds them; the warrant cannot be settled in cash and cannot be transferred, except to a successor body set up by EU law. Until a trigger event, the company's only duty is to give notice of an impending one. The purchase is completed within 20 working days of the independent valuation, and dilution can never exceed the 3 %. If a company moves its automated assets, the software, models, infrastructure, data and intellectual property its services depend on, to another company otherwise than at arm's length, or to a company under the same ownership, that other company must issue its own warrant as if it had just been designated, and the original company's duty continues for the assets it keeps. Splitting the business up this way does not increase what is owed: added together, the Reserve's total entitlement from the original company and from every company it has transferred assets to still cannot exceed 3 % of their combined fully diluted capital, however many of them there are. The same follows the assets into insolvency or restructuring: if they end up controlled by the people who controlled the original company, or by people acting together with them, the new controller must issue a fresh warrant within three months. This last rule does not catch a genuine rescue by unconnected buyers.
