# Retiring 'toetsingspoort' (26 August 2026)

The initiator read /nl/about and asked whether *toetsingspoort* is a word a
Dutch reader knows. It is not: it was a calque of *review gate* that entered
the corpus early and then propagated, including by this editor, who on 23
August standardised a stray *reviewpoorten* **onto** it because consistency
looked like correctness. The 23-August record called that fix right; it was
right about the fork and wrong about the word. Consistency arguments cannot
validate the thing they make consistent.

The replacement comes from the corpus's own native pattern, already present
in *aanwijzingstoets* and *noodzakelijkheidstoets*:

| concept | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| the six adversarial review gates | toetsingspoort(en) | toets(en) |
| the campaign's go/no-go gates | toetsingspoorten / poorten | toetsmoment(en) |
| Gate 1 (numbered campaign gate) | toetsingspoort 1 | toetsmoment 1 |
| the legal-basis gate | rechtsgrondslagpoort | rechtsgrondslagtoets |
| the layer-fidelity gate | toetsingspoort voor laaggetrouwheid | laaggetrouwheidstoets |

English shares *gate* across both concepts deliberately; Dutch now
distinguishes the standing tests on the text from the campaign's decision
moments, which is a gain, not a divergence. Twenty-three spots across seven
files; the only *poort*-forms remaining in Dutch are *poortwachter* (the DMA
term of art) and *paspoort*. The verb *spoort met* is untouched.

Fidelity gate on the changed passages against the English: PUBLISH, first
pass.

Left as is, deliberately: French *porte de contrôle* and the German and
Spanish renderings (*Prüfstufe*, *control*) were chosen by their own
native-ear rounds and are natural in those languages; the defect was
Dutch-specific.
