# Editor disposition (22 August 2026)

Objections 20 and 21 carried across into four languages, about 12 000
characters each, with the four new constraint rows. Twenty-six fixes.

## The same invented verb in two languages

The English says the Union "has lately been legislating in the other
direction". Dutch got *de Unie wetgeeft* and German got *die Union
gesetzgebt*. Neither is a word. Both were produced by the same move, forcing
an English progressive onto a noun-derived verb that does not inflect that
way in either language, and both reviewers marked them at the top of their
findings, the German as BLOCKING.

Worth recording as a pattern rather than as two typos: an English
construction with no equivalent in the target grammar produces the same
class of error in every language that shares the gap, so finding it in one
is a reason to look for it in the others rather than to fix it and move on.

## Two idioms that had to go in three languages

"Show its work", of a court asking a legislature to justify a figure, went
into French as *montrer son travail* and Spanish as *mostrar su trabajo*.
Both are BLOCKING and both are the review prompt's named failure mode: an
English idiom rendered literally is a defect even when it is grammatical. A
court asks for the reasoning to be made explicit, not for homework.

"In the same breath" went literally into French and Spanish as well. Fixed
in both.

## A German false friend worth keeping

*Erste Lesung* is the formal first-reading stage of the ordinary legislative
procedure. The English "the Legal Service will see it on the first reading"
means the first time anyone reads the document. Translating it as *erste
Lesung* put the Commission's Legal Service inside a Parliament procedure
that has not started. Now *erste Durchsicht*.

## What went wrong on this side, and what now catches it

The patch script that applies review findings re-wraps the block it edits,
because the content files are hard-wrapped. Two of the fixes landed inside
the constraints table, and a markdown table is a single block with no blank
lines in it, so the wrapper reflowed forty-six rows into one paragraph of
pipes. In German every row was destroyed; in Spanish all but one.

The rows were recoverable, because each begins with `| DC-` and ends with
its objection numbers, so they were re-split programmatically. That repair
then passed the DC-row count while the table still did not render, because
the header row had been split across two lines and the row count cannot see
it. Only counting `<table>` elements in the built HTML showed it: German and
Spanish had none, and the other three had one each.

Three things changed as a result. The patch tool now refuses to re-wrap any
block containing a line that starts with a pipe. check-translations now
fails on any table line that opens with a pipe and does not carry at least
two more, which is exactly the shape of a split row. And it was proved in
both directions: with the German header split the check exits 1 and names
the file and line, and with it restored it exits 0.

This is the second time in two days that this wrapper has silently broken
something structural, the first being four markdown URLs. Both times the
page still built and the prose still read. Both now have a check.
